# Submissions

## [Beneath Ceaseless Skies](https://beneath-ceaseless-skies.com/submissions/)

BCS wants secondary-world fantasy, strong character yearning, literary prose, and some sense of adventure or movement, preferably with a fresh setting rather than standard fantasy tropes. Here are a few story concepts that feel particularly "BCS-ish":

1. The Cartographer of Vanishing Rivers
Premise: In a desert kingdom, rivers are alive and migrate across the landscape every generation. A middle-aged mapmaker discovers that one river is deliberately fleeing something beneath the earth.
Character desire: She wants to restore her family's ruined reputation after publishing a disastrous map that led to hundreds of deaths.
Adventure element: She follows the river through forgotten canyons, ruins swallowed by sand, and territories erased from history.
Literary theme: The difference between recording truth and understanding it.
Why it fits BCS: Secondary world, unusual ecology, character-centered, exploration and wonder.

2. The Saint Who Stole Miracles
Premise: Miracles are physical objects that grow inside sacred trees. A disgraced priest steals one to save his dying daughter.
Complication: Once removed from the tree, the miracle begins transforming him into the saint who originally planted it centuries ago.
Character desire: Save his daughter while retaining his own identity.
Adventure element: A pursuit across mountain monasteries and living forests while church authorities hunt him.
Literary theme: Parentage, sacrifice, and whether goodness can be inherited.

3. Bonehouse Tide
Premise: An archipelago is built entirely on the skeleton of a sleeping sea giant. Every year bones shift, opening new passages and crushing old settlements.
Character desire: A young engineer wants to prove that the giant is not dead.
Adventure element: Expeditions into newly exposed marrow caverns.
Literary theme: Living atop histories we refuse to acknowledge.
Freshness factor: The setting itself becomes an active character.

4. The Falconer of Forgotten Wars
Premise: War memories take physical form as birds. Veterans release them after battle, but some memories become predators.
Character desire: An aging falconer seeks the memory-bird containing the last recollection of her dead wife.
Adventure element: Hunting dangerous memory-creatures across a borderland recovering from conflict.
Literary theme: Grief, remembrance, and national mythmaking.

In the aftermath of a long conflict, memories of war take physical form as birds. Veterans release them to the skies rather than carry them forever, but some memory-birds become dangerous predators, feeding on grief, fear, and old hatreds. Birria Falconer, an aging keeper of these creatures, searches for a legendary bird said to contain the last surviving memory of her dead wife. Her hunt leads her to Odd-Home, a young bovine-shaped spatial anomaly being exploited by the Teertsi as a living invasion corridor. While kingdoms view Odd-Home as a strategic threat, Birria begins to suspect the creature remembers more than anyone realizes.

Birria Falconer has spent decades hunting memory-birds across the scarred borderlands left behind by forgotten wars. These strange creatures are born when veterans release memories they can no longer bear to carry, and while most eventually dissipate, some become feral predators that feed on grief, guilt, and old hatreds. Birria is renowned for capturing and calming such birds, but her current quest is deeply personal: she seeks a rare memory-bird believed to carry the final recollection of her late wife, a memory she fears may otherwise be lost forever. Over the years, her hunt has revealed an uncomfortable truth: the memories preserved by the birds often contradict the heroic stories nations tell about themselves.

As rumors spread of the nascent anomaly known as Odd-Home, Birria becomes convinced that the creature is connected to the disappearance of certain memories and perhaps even to the elusive bird she seeks. While rulers and generals view Odd-Home as a military threat or a strategic asset, Birria sees signs of suffering and confusion. The Teertsi invaders appear to be exploiting the young anomaly, feeding it timber from the ancient Mithwood to sustain a growing portal that could transport armies across the frontier. Yet Birria discovers that Odd-Home is not nourished by wood alone. The rings of the Mithwood contain only simple traces of seasons and years, while memories of living beings contain the emotional richness that Odd-Home truly craves.

To understand the anomaly, Birria recruits a young mage student whose only magical talent is the ability to lighten his own body and drift through the air. Ridiculed by his peers, he has long dreamed of proving himself worthy. Birria realizes that his strange gift makes him uniquely suited to explore Odd-Home's gravityless interior, a shifting landscape of forgotten experiences, lost histories, and fragmented identities. As the two form an unlikely partnership, they uncover the truth that Odd-Home is neither weapon nor portal but a living being searching for its own forgotten past, much as Birria searches for her wife and the student searches for a place where he belongs.

The story's central conflict emerges when the recovery of Odd-Home's memories threatens to disrupt the political balance of the region. The more the anomaly remembers itself, the less willing it becomes to serve as a corridor for armies. At the same time, the memories preserved within it challenge cherished national legends, revealing uncomfortable truths about heroism, sacrifice, and responsibility. Birria must ultimately decide whether to pursue the final memory of her wife, potentially awakening Odd-Home and exposing those truths, or protect the myths that bring comfort to thousands. Through grief, friendship, and remembrance, the story explores how individuals and nations alike construct identity from the stories they choose to remember and the ones they choose to forget.

6. A Market for Lost Names
Premise: Names can be bought and sold. The poorer you are, the more pieces of your identity you surrender.
Character desire: A clerk who can no longer remember her own childhood seeks the merchant who purchased her original name decades earlier.
Adventure element: Journey through trading cities where identity has become currency.
Literary theme: Selfhood, class, and memory.

7. The Last Dragon Dentist
Premise: Dragons are extinct except for one ancient dragon whose teeth generate the magical metals that power civilization.
Character desire: The dragon's caretaker wants to free it before it dies from centuries of exploitation.
Adventure element: Smuggling a mountain-sized dragon beyond the borders of empire.
Literary theme: Industrialization and ethical stewardship.

8. The Horns of a Dilemma
Premise: A mage schools worst student proves himself when he befriends a spatial anomaly which is paid to transport an invading force.
Character desire: The student wants to prove himself, but really, he also feels lonely and wants to make a friend.
Adventure element: The student uses his magic to float inside the anomaly to discover how it works.
Literary theme: Memories of things past.

A struggling mage student at The Oakenshield Huild possesses a seemingly useless talent: he can lighten himself and drift like a balloon. When a nascent spatial anomaly called Odd-Home, a gentle bovine-like being inspired by Auðhumla, appears above the three towns of the Delta, many believe it heralds a planned invasion by the Teertsi. The student's friend and mentor, an empath named Birria Falconer, feels that the portal is distressed rather than monstrous. She also believes the anomaly may hold traces of memories connected to her estranged wife, and convinces the student to use his unusual magic to float into Odd-Home's impossible interior and discover what she truly is.

Inside Odd-Home, the student expects to uncover the mechanics of a portal, but instead discovers a living repository of memories. The Teertsi have been force-feeding her timber from the ancient Mithwood, believing it fuels her ability to transport armies. In reality, the tree rings contain only primitive fragments of memory, enough to sustain her existence but not enough to restore her identity. Odd-Home has forgotten herself and hungers not merely for memories, but for meaningful connections between memories. As the student explores her dreamlike interior, he realizes that she is less a portal than a creature of remembrance, collecting echoes of lives, stories, and forgotten moments.

The story's emotional core centers on three lonely beings seeking connection. The student wants recognition but truly longs for friendship; his friend seeks answers about a loved one lost to the past; and Odd-Home seeks the missing pieces of her own identity. The student's talent, mocked by everyone else, becomes uniquely suited to navigating Odd-Home's gravityless interior. Through their growing bond, he learns that worth comes not from power but from empathy. Ultimately, he faces a dilemma involving the fate of Odd-Home, the Teertsi invasion, and the memories his friend desperately seeks, forcing him to choose between achievement, truth, and loyalty. The central theme is that memories alone are not enough; meaning emerges when memories are connected to one another through relationships, stories, and acts of compassion.

My favorite for BCS

The Falconer of Forgotten Wars.

It has:

a distinctive fantasy premise,
a deeply personal emotional core,
room for lush prose,
an external quest,
and a protagonist yearning for something impossible.

That's very much the kind of combination BCS often seems to favor: the adventure matters because of the character's heart, not the other way around.
